# Stage 3 workstream 3.4: the Fast contract on the 172 pinned guns

Refs #40; ADR 0013 sections 6 and 7 ("Fast does not get exact Gate A/B/C or physical infeasibility by declaration"); stage 2 decisions 2a, 2b, 2c;
`docs/stage3-plan.md` row 3.4.

## Verdict

- **Every published Fast witness validates.** 612 Fast builds over 4 profiles x 172 guns. The production validator accepts every one (path-derived
  instance ids required, the request's hard constraints, TED included). No contract violation, no error.
- **Every Fast outcome is truthfully qualified.** 688 Fast responses: 612 `optimal / approximate_optimal` and 76 `incomplete / none`
  ("Approximate model infeasible"). All are resolved `fast` / `approximate`, none has a physical upper bound or gap, and **none carries
  `certified_optimal` or `certified_infeasible` or status `infeasible`**. `single_copy` is present exactly on the single-copy profiles, and no
  single-copy build repeats a part.
- **Agreement with the certified Precise optimum (not a contract requirement; Fast is approximate):** Fast reached the certified optimum score
  (within the 1e-5 tolerance) on all 153 guns Precise certifies optimal, in all four profiles, plain and TrueErgo. Repeats profiles also returned
  the identical build every time. Single-copy builds matched it 127 (plain) and 133 (TrueErgo) times; the rest are ties with a different build. On
  the 19 guns Precise certifies infeasible, Fast reports `incomplete / none` (Approximate model infeasible) in every profile. That is the required
  qualification, and it is never an infeasibility claim. Fast never scored above or below a certified optimum.
- No bug found.

## Setup

- Production `solve()` (`src/solver/solver.ts`), `preciseMode: false`, `precisionRequest: 'fast'`, 60 s `timeLimitMs` per request. The request is
  Phase 2.10's: weights e 1 / r 1 / p 0.1, flea, all traders level 4, player level 50, default tolerance 1e-5.
- Profiles: `fast-plain-single` (default, one copy per part), `fast-plain-repeats` (`allowRepeatedParts: true`), `fast-ted-single` and
  `fast-ted-repeats` (`useTrueErgo: true`). Yardsticks: `precise-plain` and `precise-ted` on the same request. Precise always allows repeats.
- Contract check: `tests/physical/fastContract.check.ts` (shared with the unit tests).
- Snapshot `frontend/benchmarks/snapshot.json`, SHA-256 `219443b449f732f3702cd9a706e82646dfe0c2d0741900700d0d10cbd33b235c` (checked by the script).
  Code: worktree tip `757bc7d` (no `src/` changes). Node v26.7.0, the pinned native HiGHS build. 8 shards on a 16-core machine shared with other
  agents' runs, so wall times are under contention.
- Reproduce (from `frontend/`): `benchmarks/stage3-fast/run.sh benchmarks/stage3-fast/results/run1 8`, then
  `npx tsx benchmarks/stage3-fast/summarize.ts benchmarks/stage3-fast/results/run1`. Raw JSONL is gitignored (`stage3-fast/results/`).

## Timing note

Fast TrueErgo totals 442 to 450 s against 980 s for Precise TrueErgo; the max is 21 s against 55 s (AK family, ADAR, TX-15). Fast plain totals 43 to 50 s
against 93 s for Precise plain. Both modes return the same optimum here. Auto's rule (performance policy item 1) picks Fast only for TrueErgo/Prevent
Overswing above 3,000 candidate edges, and those guns are the slowest ones above.

## Unit tests added (same contract, small worlds)

- `tests/physical/fastContract.ledger.test.ts`: every ledger case (149) through `solve()` in Fast mode, 6 variants: the case's own request, TrueErgo
  forced, Prevent Overswing forced, each single copy and repeats. That is 894 solves. Each build is checked by the production validator and by the
  reference validator (`validateReference` via the harness decoding). Oracle-empty cases get no build and `incomplete / none`. The 5 cyclic cases are
  refused in every variant.
- `tests/physical/fastContract.worlds.test.ts`: plain seeded worlds 150 x 2 copy modes, TED worlds 200 x 2, worlds from the cyclic generator
  40 x 2 x 2, interrupted searches (cancel at the k-th yield, past deadline: 440 solves, including `feasible / feasible` "Cancelled by user"
  witnesses), and a tamper self-test showing the checker catches each kind of violation.
